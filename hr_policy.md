# HR Leave Policy

## Paid leave (congés payés)
Employees earn 2.5 days of paid leave per month worked, so 30 days per year (5 weeks), counted in working days from Monday to Saturday (jours ouvrables). A month that is only partly worked, such as the month someone joins or leaves, earns a share of the 2.5 days in proportion to the days worked. This is exactly the legal minimum in France (Code du travail, article L3141-3); the company does not give less. Paid leave is separate from casual leave and sick leave. It is normally taken between 1 May and 31 October, and is requested through your manager.

## Casual leave
Employees get 12 casual leave days per year. Casual leave must be applied at least 2 working days in advance, except in emergencies. A maximum of 5 consecutive casual leave days can be taken at once.

## Sick leave
Employees get 6 sick leave days per year. Sick leave of more than 2 consecutive days requires a medical certificate, uploaded within 3 days of returning to work.

## Carry forward
Up to 5 unused casual leave days can be carried forward to the next year. Unused sick leave cannot be carried forward and lapses on 31 December. Unused paid leave is not lost at the end of the year: it is carried forward in full, and at the start of each year the total is rounded up to the next whole day (Code du travail, article L3141-7).

## Public holidays
The company follows the published public holiday calendar. If a public holiday falls during approved leave, that day is not counted as leave.

## Work from home
Employees may work from home up to 2 days per week with manager approval. Work from home is not counted as leave.

## Notice period
The notice period is 30 days for employees and 60 days for managers. Leave cannot be taken during the notice period without HR approval.

## Payslips
Payslips are issued on the last working day of each month. Employees can view their own payslips through the HR assistant. Questions about errors in a payslip should be raised with HR.
